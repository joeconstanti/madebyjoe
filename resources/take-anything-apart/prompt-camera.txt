Take a 35mm film SLR camera apart for me in 3D. One file: index.html. Three.js from a CDN, no build step, no downloaded models: make every part from geometry in code.

1. Research first: how a 35mm film SLR camera works and what its real parts are. Use a standard layout. Tell me at the end which parts and numbers you could not confirm.
2. It must run. Every moving part moves the way it does in real life, at the right relative speed.
3. An "explode" slider. At 0 it is the finished object. At 1 every part floats apart along its real assembly axis, in order, still moving.
4. Click any part: the camera flies to it, the other parts fade, and a card gives its name, what it does and one number that matters. A breadcrumb lets me go back out.
5. Let me go down to the mirror and the shutter curtain and see how it works.
6. A "Tour" button: one smooth 40 second camera move. Start on the finished object, take it apart, dive to the mirror and the shutter curtain, then put it back together.
7. Make it look like a product film: dark background, studio lighting with reflections (RoomEnvironment or a generated environment map), real-looking materials, shallow depth of field if it stays fast. It must work in a 1080 x 1080 window and on a phone.
8. The page must take ?tour=1 and ?explode=0.6.

Serve it locally, test it in a real browser with Playwright, look at the screenshots at several explode values and during the tour, and fix what looks wrong before you say it is done.
